1c:Tb78,<p>This page lists every correction Signal to Decision has issued, newest first. Nothing is removed from it
once published.</p>
<p><strong>The register has been open since 19 July 2026.</strong> It existed before its first entry, on purpose: a
corrections page that appears only on the day of the first correction is not a standing practice.</p>
<h3 id="26-november-2026-the-july-2026-edition">26 November 2026: the July 2026 edition</h3>
<p>Four statements in the July edition were wrong when it was published.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>MCP.</strong> The edition said a twelve-month timeline had been issued to standardise HTTP+SSE transport and
dynamic registration. The July revision started a twelve-month clock to retire those features.</li>
<li><strong>Kimi K3 licence.</strong> The edition merged two conditions into one. A hosted-model service with more than
$20 million of revenue in any twelve months needs a separate agreement with Moonshot. A product with
more than 100 million monthly active users, or more than $20 million of monthly revenue, must display
"Kimi K3".</li>
<li><strong>Insurers.</strong> The edition stated as a market-wide fact that insurers had begun carving AI out of standard
cover. The source is one legal alert, about a standard insurance form, and the claim is now attributed
to it.</li>
<li><strong>Eighteen years.</strong> The edition said "eighteen years". The correct figure is more than eighteen years.</li>
</ul>
<p>Corrected in the edition's archive on this site and in the edition PDF, each with a dated note.</p>
<h3 id="how-corrections-work-here">How corrections work here</h3>
<p>If Signal to Decision publishes a statement of fact that is wrong, the correction runs at the top of the
next edition, above the first story. Not in a footer, and not only on the web.</p>
<p>The correction issues on every surface the error reached. If it went out on this site, in the LinkedIn
edition, in the video and in the audio, it is corrected on every one of them.</p>
<p>The note names what was wrong, not only what is now right, and it says how the error happened. The word
used is "correction". Not "update", not "clarifies". Where the archive page carried the error, that page
is corrected and an amendment note is appended to it, so the record shows both the fix and the fact that a
fix was needed.</p>
<p>Something published accurately that later changes is not a correction. It gets a dated note saying what
changed and when.</p>
<h3 id="telling-me-about-an-error">Telling me about an error</h3>
<p>If you think something here is wrong, tell me and I will check it against the primary source. If it is
wrong, it gets corrected on this page and in the next edition. If it is right, I will show you the source.</p>
