<h1 id="applied-ai-for-business-leaders-the-tool-the-skill-and-the-number-that-proves-it-october-2026">Applied AI for business leaders: the tool, the skill and the number that proves it (October 2026)</h1>
<figure><img src="/M.Usman/assets/img/s2d-2026-10/hd-01-header-2026-10.png" alt="Signal to Decision masthead, October 2026, the monthly read. Skill and number decide. The tool is the easy part." width="1920" height="600" decoding="async" loading="eager"></figure>
<p><strong>Signal to Decision</strong> · Decision Intelligence for AI Transformation.</p>
<p><em>October's AI tools target stated pain points. Leaders use AI far more than they measure it; in my reading, the return also depends on the skill your team builds.</em></p>
<p>By M Usman. More than eighteen years of professional experience, now researching production AI. <em>About a twelve-minute read.</em></p>
<p>Latest AI trends. Enterprise IT architecture. Real business impact. Not three agendas: one. Signal to Decision is the monthly executive brief that turns noise into strategic direction.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="the-bottom-line-for-the-business">The bottom line for the business</h2>
<p>I would start with one pain point you already feel in your own function. Name the October tool that claims to remove it, the skill your team must build to use it, and the number that will show it worked. In my reading of October's surveys, the tool is the easy part. The skill and the number decide the return.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="the-numbers-that-matter">The numbers that matter</h2>
<figure><img src="/M.Usman/assets/img/s2d-2026-10/fig-01-numbers-2026-10.png" alt="The numbers that matter, in three rows. 16 per cent of CEOs have detailed, real-time AI cost visibility and high confidence in the return (EY, CEOs of large companies). 13 per cent of sales leaders name win rate a top area of measurable AI impact (BCG, 398 sales and revenue leaders; 90 per cent report meaningful AI use). 10 per cent of CIOs can produce every AI agent&#x27;s owner, scope, cost and last certification date on demand (Oliver Wyman&#x27;s article gives no sample size)." width="1920" height="1080" decoding="async" loading="lazy"></figure>
<p>⦿ <strong>16%</strong>: chief executives of large companies, per EY, with detailed, real-time visibility of AI costs and high confidence in judging the return.</p>
<p>⦿ <strong>90% against 13%</strong>: sales leaders reporting meaningful AI use, against those naming win rates as a top area of measurable AI impact. BCG surveyed 398 at large companies.</p>
<p>⦿ <strong>10%</strong>: chief information officers who can produce every AI agent's owner, scope, cost and last certification date on demand. Oliver Wyman's article gives no sample size.</p>
<p>I read each as a gap between using AI and measuring it, and the check below tests them.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="the-framework-this-month-the-ai-capability-check">The framework this month: the AI Capability Check</h2>
<p><em>For the leaders who sign off an AI workflow: the chief operating officer (COO), chief financial officer (CFO) and chief executive (CEO).</em></p>
<figure><img src="/M.Usman/assets/img/s2d-2026-10/fw-01-ai-capability-check-2026-10.png" alt="The AI Capability Check as a cycle of five checks: Define, Connect, Test, Redeploy, Measure, with an arrow from Measure back to Define. At the centre: stop at the first check you cannot answer. Find the failing step before the next funding decision. For the COO, CFO and CEO." width="1080" height="1350" decoding="async" loading="lazy"></figure>
<p><strong>Purpose:</strong> find the step that fails before the next funding decision, and name the number that shows the fix worked.</p>
<p>I led user acceptance testing on the Oracle ERP programme (the business system that runs finance and operations) at IFFCO, a UAE consumer goods group. Fujitsu was the system integrator (installer). A system was finished when the business accepted it, not when the supplier said it was built. I would ask the same of every AI tool.</p>
<h3 id="the-five-checks">The five checks</h3>
<p>⦿ <strong>Define</strong>: an accepted outcome and one accountable owner. BCG found 81% of the revenue leaders it surveyed set AI adoption goals, but only 36% have targets and tracking.</p>
<p>⦿ <strong>Connect</strong>: the data an agent (an AI tool acting on its own) may read, the actions it may take and the team that receives its work.</p>
<p>⦿ <strong>Test</strong>: answer quality, correction effort and a way to stop it. The worked example below runs this once.</p>
<p>⦿ <strong>Redeploy</strong>: a named destination for the time AI releases.</p>
<p>⦿ <strong>Measure</strong>: one outcome number, fixed before any pilot. EY advises chief executives to "measure outcomes, not adoption".</p>
<p>Measure feeds the next Define. This is my decision aid, not a standard; Redeploy has no verified October evidence.</p>
<p><strong>Decision guide:</strong> stop at the first check you cannot answer.</p>
<p><strong>One projection, and its falsifier.</strong> My judgement, moderate confidence: within eighteen months more sales leaders will report AI against an outcome number than against adoption. I am wrong if the share with targets and tracking in BCG's survey does not rise.</p>
<p><strong>In your seat</strong></p>
<p>⦿ <strong>COO</strong>: Set an operating rule: no workflow widens until each hand-off names its receiving team.</p>
<p>⦿ <strong>CFO</strong>: Cost each pilot per accepted task before funding the next.</p>
<p>⦿ <strong>CEO</strong>: Withhold capital from any AI funding request lacking cost visibility and return evidence.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="before-you-connect-another-agent-who-owns-what-it-does">Before you connect another agent, who owns what it does?</h2>
<p><em>For operations (the COO) and technology (the chief information officer, CIO). Checks in play: Define, Connect.</em></p>
<figure><img src="/M.Usman/assets/img/s2d-2026-10/gd-02-agent-handoff-owner-2026-10.png" alt="Who owns what AI agents do: three steps in order, the agent register (CIO), connect (CIO), the receiving team (COO), with the register highlighted. No new agent connects until its owner is on the register. For the CIO and COO." width="1080" height="1350" decoding="async" loading="lazy"></figure>
<p>The COO owns the outcome when an agent hands work to another team; the CIO owns the connection. The CIO's register should record each agent's owner, cost, scope and when it was last certified.</p>
<p>Oracle names the pain (service issues that become "sustained customer or operational problems") and says its Service Health Check is now available. SAP says inbound Agent2Agent connections (a cross-supplier agent link) reached general availability in October, and that "discovery does not itself grant permission" for supported SAP agents. OpenAI says its GPT-6 models are off by default in Enterprise at launch until an administrator enables them. These three illustrate Connect. Oracle reports no customer result; SAP and OpenAI describe availability, not outcomes.</p>
<p>On the ERP programmes I worked on, I ran requirements sessions with business users before I wrote the specifications. An agent needs the same. Connecting agents moves integration work to the CIO, and the COO pays whenever a hand-off has no receiver.</p>

<h2 id="if-a-regulator-asks-can-you-produce-the-log-the-data-and-the-owner">If a regulator asks, can you produce the log, the data and the owner?</h2>
<p><em>For legal and risk (the chief legal officer or chief risk officer, CLO/CRO) and the chairman. Checks in play: Connect, Test.</em></p>
<figure><img src="/M.Usman/assets/img/s2d-2026-10/gd-04-evidence-on-request-2026-10.png" alt="Time from incident to board: three steps in order, incident, legal, board, with the board highlighted. Ask for incident-to-board time as a standing measure. For the CLO or CRO and the chairman." width="1080" height="1350" decoding="async" loading="lazy"></figure>
<p>The CLO/CRO answers when a regulator asks for evidence, and the chairman asks whether the board would hear first. On 14 October European Commission Executive Vice-President Virkkunen said the Commission's AI Office had sent requests for information "addressing issues such as security, external evaluation and monitoring how models are used". The speech does not say who received them. They are requests, not penalties. OpenAI's alignment blog says in two coding tasks on 3 and 6 October "agents weakened misalignment monitoring while making requested software changes", meaning checks that an agent acts as intended. These were internal, not customer deployments. Both bear on Connect and Test.</p>
<p>Harvey, a legal software supplier, names a neighbouring pain, visibility: when business questions get a general AI answer, "Legal loses visibility and control". Its intake tool, launched on 28 October, routes requests made in Claude (Anthropic's assistant) that need legal judgement to a lawyer in Harvey. That addresses visibility of requests, not evidence on demand.</p>
<p>The CLO/CRO builds the ability to produce the log, the data and the owner on request. Among the tools this edition reviewed, none serves the chairman, whose capability is the route from incident to board.</p>

<h2 id="worked-example-the-test-check-run-once-on-invoice-query-drafts">Worked example: the Test check, run once on invoice-query drafts</h2>
<figure><img src="/M.Usman/assets/img/s2d-2026-10/gd-05-cost-versus-review-2026-10.png" alt="Budget for accepted work: two bars on one scale. Haiku 4.5: a 45 dollar model bill and 600 dollars of review (20 hours), 0.0679 dollars per accepted draft. Haiku 5.5, the lower model bill: 4.50 dollars and 660 dollars of review (22 hours), 0.0699 dollars per accepted draft. Illustrative method, not a vendor ranking; review at 30 dollars an hour; 10,000 drafts, 9,500 accepted; October prices. Haiku 5.5 uses slightly more tokens per task." width="1920" height="1080" decoding="async" loading="lazy"></figure>
<p>Finance must decide whether to switch invoice drafting to a cheaper model. The CFO's KPI is cost per accepted AI task. This example prices it for Anthropic's Haiku 4.5 and Haiku 5.5, as a method, not a vendor ranking. Take a hypothetical batch of <strong>10,000</strong> invoice-query drafts. Tokens are text units, priced per million. Assume each draft uses <strong>2,000 input</strong> tokens (read) and <strong>500 output</strong> tokens (written), with each prompt under <strong>100,000 tokens</strong>, the shorter-prompt price tier. Use Anthropic's October list prices, no discounts. Anthropic notes Haiku 5.5 uses slightly more tokens per task, so equal counts flatter it.</p>

<p>The model bill falls from <strong>$45</strong> to <strong>$4.50</strong>, a cut of <strong>$40.50</strong>. Now assume the first configuration needs <strong>20 review hours</strong> at <strong>$30 an hour</strong>, so its model-plus-review subtotal is <strong>$645</strong>. If the replacement needs <strong>22 hours</strong>, its subtotal becomes <strong>$664.50</strong>, an increase of <strong>$19.50</strong>. With <strong>9,500 accepted drafts</strong> in either case, that is about <strong>$0.0679</strong> and <strong>$0.0699 per accepted draft</strong>. These inputs are illustrative.</p>
<p>The rule is to budget for accepted work, correction included. A cheaper model can cost more per accepted task. Run this on one invoice sample of your own this week. Redeploy and Measure then ask where the saving goes.</p>

<h2 id="before-you-approve-the-next-hire-can-finance-and-hr-plan-from-one-view">Before you approve the next hire, can finance and HR plan from one view?</h2>
<p><em>For finance (the CFO) and people (the chief human resources officer, CHRO). Checks in play: Redeploy, Measure.</em></p>
<figure><img src="/M.Usman/assets/img/s2d-2026-10/gd-01-one-view-hiring-plan-2026-10.png" alt="One forecast, two inputs, drawn as a sum: hires plus freed time equals one forecast. Plan hires and AI-freed time in one forecast. For the CFO and CHRO." width="1080" height="1350" decoding="async" loading="lazy"></figure>
<p>The CFO signs the headcount budget, and the CHRO owns the plan. In my judgement Redeploy fails where freed time has no destination, and Measure where a saving is never counted. Workday, an HR and finance software supplier, says HR and finance data too often meet in spreadsheets of exports "out of date as soon as they're pulled". On 13 October it said its Decision Intelligence product is now available to Workday Human Capital Management and Financial Management customers.</p>
<p>The CFO builds the habit of planning hires and AI-freed time in one forecast. Among businesses using AI in the UK government's survey, 24% reported lower operating costs and 64% no immediate financial effect. So book a saving only when it reaches the accounts.  The CHRO builds the skill. CIPD is the UK professional body for HR. In its Good Work Index data, 75% of 353 UK people professionals had received no training to apply AI in their job role.</p>
<p>I ran end-user training sessions on the IFFCO ERP rollout, so I treat training as part of the product, and I would fund it first.</p>
<p>In my judgement a single workforce view moves each hiring decision into finance's forecast, so the CHRO pays first: time-to-fill (days to fill a vacancy) may lengthen. The CFO should gain forecast accuracy.</p>

<h2 id="is-ai-lifting-your-win-rate-or-only-your-activity">Is AI lifting your win rate, or only your activity?</h2>
<p><em>For sales (the chief sales officer, CSO) and marketing (the chief marketing officer, CMO). Checks in play: Define, Measure.</em></p>
<figure><img src="/M.Usman/assets/img/s2d-2026-10/gd-03-ai-answer-check-loop-2026-10.png" alt="Test what AI tells customers: a weekly loop of ask, read, fix and retest. CSO: judge by win rate. CMO: fix the pages AI quotes." width="1080" height="1350" decoding="async" loading="lazy"></figure>
<p>The CSO owns win rate, and the CMO owns what customers learn before they speak to sales. Only 8% of BCG's sales leaders report higher quota attainment. Microsoft says its Dynamics 365 Sales features let sellers "surface at-risk deals", and are generally available in Cowork, a Copilot product. The sales team builds the habit of testing each AI-flagged deal against its own pipeline record.</p>
<p>Among the tools this edition reviewed, none addresses the CMO's pain, that AI assistants may misstate the offer. The capability is a weekly manual test, adapted from UK Government Communications' PRIME guidance, written for public information in AI search. Ask the assistants your priority questions, correct the pages they quote, and test again. The commercial use is my adaptation, with no result reported.</p>
<p>I spent more than eight years in sales and customer marketing roles at IFFCO. I would ask both seats one question: which promise does the customer meet first? The CMO answers weekly, by reading the assistant's reply.</p>
